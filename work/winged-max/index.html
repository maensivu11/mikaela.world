the idea

the deciding part of dating apps takes seconds. the swiping, the reading, the opening lines and finding a thursday that works take hours. winged max does the hours, and you keep the seconds.

it's a macos app that mirrors your iphone and works the dating apps inside that window. no scraping and no fake account: it's your hinge, on your phone, under your account, driven by something that learned what you like, where you can watch it.

winged sets up your dates over text and calls. max brings the same deal to your own phone screen: the agent does the labor, you decide.

earned, and taken back

autonomy is a ladder, not a toggle.

the agent starts with no permission at all. after enough ratings, a small model is fitted on your mac, and auto-like and auto-pass unlock only if it predicts choices it was never shown better than chance. it acts only at the confident ends, under a daily cap, and if the model stops holding up, autopilot goes back off and the app tells you.

public var unlocksAgent: Bool {
    validationCount >= 50 && auc > 0.6
}

the unlock rule, from the code

a model graded on profiles it trained on can pass by memorizing you, so it's graded only on ones it never saw. you can't switch trust on. it has to be earned.

WingedMaxCore/RatingWeightPolicy.swift
/// A correction is the user deliberately overruling the agent,
/// which is the most informative thing they ever do. And taste
/// moves, so a rating from months ago describes who they were,
/// not who they are.
public func weight(ageDays: Double, corrected: Bool) -> Double {
    let decayed = pow(0.5, age / halfLifeDays)1
    let recency = max(decayed, minimumWeight)2
    return recency * (corrected ? correctionMultiplier : 1)3
}

1120-day half-life   2floor 0.15   3a correction counts three times

corrections count triple

training refits on every rating, and listens hardest to corrections.

watch mode, "learn while i browse," turns a habit you already have into training. you browse hinge as usual, and winged captures each profile and asks three quick things: your first reaction, like or pass, and why.

"not my type" and "wrong life plans" are separate answers, so passing on someone who doesn't want kids never teaches the model you don't find them attractive.

a half-life with a floor keeps your early ratings as a quiet vote instead of forgetting them on a cutoff date.

approve is not send

drafts are review-first. sending fails closed.

the agent writes in your voice. three style answers (emoji, formality, reply length) shape every draft, and your own writing samples teach the rest. no message leaves the mac without you.

this is the call i'd defend to anyone. a wrong message costs trust, in your name, to a real person, so approving a draft never sends it.

my design laws for agentic products

i design by these now.

show the agent's conclusions, not just its inputs. if people train something, they need to see what it learned and correct it. that surface is the loop.

only ask for input you'll use. every question you ask spends the goodwill training runs on.

honest state over optimistic state. "connected" goes false when it's false, and a counter counts the thing it names.

price the work up front. training takes about an hour of careful rating before anything changes, so the first screen says so.
