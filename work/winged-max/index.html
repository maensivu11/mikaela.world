winged max

winged max is the agent running entirely on your own mac. it mirrors your iphone, learns your taste, and acts on your behalf locally.

i'm the design engineer & founder. i designed it and built it.

stage: working build, unfinished interface. this page shows how it works, not how it looks.

the idea

dating apps are a second job. the deciding part takes seconds. the swiping, the reading, the opening lines and finding a thursday that works take hours. so the pitch was short: an agent that does the labor, while you stay the one who decides.

winged max is a macos app that mirrors your iphone on your mac and operates the dating apps inside that window. no scraping, no fake account. it's your hinge, on your phone, under your account, driven by something that learned what you like, where you can watch it work.

the deciding part takes seconds. the rest takes hours. max is built around that gap, and the decision stays on your side of it.
before max, winged was the agent that texts and calls to set up your dates. max takes the same split into your own phone screen. the agent does the labor, you decide.
the interface isn't finished and i'm not going to dress it up. the part that has to be right first is the mechanism: what the agent may do, when it earns more, and what it can never do. a pretty screen on top of a wrong permission is the worst version of this product. so this page shows the permissions.

how the agent works

an agent that sees who you find attractive and writes as you has to earn trust mechanically, not with copy. three rules, each enforced in code: the human confirms anything consequential, autonomy is earned and revocable, and your taste stays yours.

  1. 1mirroryour iPhone's screen in a window on the Mac
  2. 2capture + stitchscroll the profile, join it into one image
  3. 3read facts · cloudage, height, prompts read from the screenshot
  4. 4hard filtersage, distance, smoking, drinking, children: real vetoes
  5. 5score · on the Mac0.8 your taste model + 0.2 your stated preferences
  6. 6act or asklike, pass, or hand it back to you

you can watch it work

it works inside your real app, where you can watch.

winged max drives apple's iphone mirroring, so the agent sees and taps the same window you would. that was a product choice as much as a technical one: an agent you can watch is one you can trust with your account. only one step leaves the mac, reading facts off a profile screenshot. i documented exactly what leaves and what doesn't.

mirroring needs macos 15 and isn't available in the eu, so availability, not capture, was the real constraint.

earned, and taken back

autonomy is a ladder, not a toggle.

the agent starts with no permission at all. after enough ratings a small model is fitted on the mac, and it only unlocks auto-like and auto-pass if it predicts choices it was never shown better than chance. even then it acts only at the confident ends, under a daily cap. if the model goes invalid, autopilot is taken away and the app says so.

public var unlocksAgent: Bool {
    validationCount >= 50 && auc > 0.6
}

AttractionClassifier.swift · every rating contributes one prediction from a model that never saw it

a model graded on profiles it trained on can pass by memorizing you. grading it only on ones it never saw is the honest test. a toggle would skip the test entirely, and an agent that can be switched on can't be earned.

WingedMaxCore/RatingWeightPolicy.swift
/// A correction is the user deliberately overruling the agent,
/// which is the most informative thing they ever do. And taste
/// moves, so a rating from months ago describes who they were,
/// not who they are.
public func weight(ageDays: Double, corrected: Bool) -> Double {
    let decayed = pow(0.5, age / halfLifeDays)1
    let recency = max(decayed, minimumWeight)2
    return recency * (corrected ? correctionMultiplier : 1)3
}

1120-day half-life   2floor 0.15   3a correction counts three times

corrections count triple

training refits on every rating, and listens hardest to corrections.

watch mode ("learn while i browse") turns a habit you already have into training. you browse hinge as usual. winged captures each profile as you scroll and asks three short questions: your visual reaction, like or pass, and a reason.

i separated "not my type" from "wrong life plans", so passing on someone who doesn't want kids never teaches the model you don't find them attractive.

a half-life with a floor, not a cutoff: a hard window would forget your long-standing taste the day it turned old. the floor keeps the early ratings as a quiet vote. and a correction counts triple because the moment you overrule the agent is the only moment it learns it was wrong.

1until delivery can be proved, "send" is an error, not an action · excerpt, body shortened

approve is not send

drafts are review-first. sending fails closed.

the agent writes in your voice. three style answers (emoji, formality, reply length) are enforced on every draft, and writing samples or a consent-gated scan of your own past messages teach the rest. but in the build i audited, no message could leave the machine. approving a draft is not sending it, and the code refuses to pretend otherwise.

until delivery can be proved, an agent that says "sent" might be lying to you. an error is honest. a fake success isn't.

this is the call i'd defend to anyone. no message leaves without you. a wrong like costs nothing. a wrong message costs trust, in your name, to a real person. approving a draft is not sending it, and i won't let the code pretend it is.

the 62 tasks by area, one scale

three code auditors ran in parallel, each in its own lane: onboarding and connection, agent logic, and a simplicity sweep against my own style guide. a fourth went under the ui into the mirroring layer. every finding needed a file and line. anything only provable on a live phone was labeled a hypothesis. a second round caught the app screenshotting and calling paid inference every 5 seconds on a static screen, about 720 calls an hour.

the auditors were ai agents reading the repo. i set the lanes, the fixtures and the bar: my thirteen defects first, a file and line for every finding, and anything only provable on a live phone marked a hypothesis. they found things. i decided what mattered.
  • every claim has a receipt, every guess is labeled a guess
  • one defect, one task, one plain sentence anyone on the team can pick up
  • keep the design calls that are actually mine, and hand off the rest
what came out was a board, not a report. 62 tasks, each a plain verb with a root cause and a proposed owner. i kept one for myself as design direction: rebuilding matching preferences as one page. the order was safety first.

my design laws for agentic products

tracing the training loop left me with these. i design by them now.

show the agent's conclusions, not just its inputs. if people train something, they need to see what it learned and correct it. that surface is the loop.

never ask for input you won't use. fifty forced justifications that nothing reads burn exactly the goodwill training depends on.

honest state beats optimistic state. "connected" has to go false when it's false. a counter has to count the thing it names.

price the work up front. training is roughly an hour of careful rating before anything changes. say so on the first screen.

i carried these into the agentic products i built after this one. if the agent concludes something about a person, show it and let them argue with it.
