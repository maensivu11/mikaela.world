winged max

winged max is a mac app that does your dating app swiping for you. it learns who you're into from the profiles you like and pass on, then swipes for you on your own phone, right where you can watch, and it never messages anyone without your yes.

design engineer & founder. i designed it and made it agentic.

this page shows how it works under the hood: what the agent may do for you, and when.

where it started

winged started as an agent that sets up your dates over text and calls. but most people already have a dating app on their phone, with their matches and their history in it.

winged max takes the same deal into that app: the agent does the labor and you make the calls. it's your hinge, on your phone, under your own account. no scraping and no fake profile.

it runs on your mac. what it learns about your taste, your ratings and your dating history stay there, on a machine you own.

the problem

dating apps are a second job. the deciding part takes seconds: you know in a moment if someone is your type. the rest takes hours: the swiping, the reading, the opening lines, finding a thursday that works.

handing that off usually means handing your love life to something you can't see, a bot on someone's server or a fake account. winged max does the hours in plain sight, on your own phone screen, and it only takes on more once it has shown it knows your taste.

the deciding part takes seconds. the rest takes hours. max is built around that gap, and the decision stays on your side of it.

how it works

seven steps, from plugging in your phone to waking up to a morning summary.

1. connect your iphone. max shows your iphone on your mac screen using apple's iphone mirroring, the same window you could open yourself.

2. set your deal-breakers. age range, distance, smoking, drinking, kids. these are hard rules: max never likes someone who breaks one.

3. browse like you always do. you swipe through hinge as usual. max looks at each profile with you and asks three quick things: your first reaction, like or pass, and why.

4. it learns your type. every answer teaches a small model on your mac what you're drawn to. what you actually pick counts more than what you say you want.

5. it earns autopilot. once it can correctly guess choices it was never shown, you can let it like and pass for you, a few a day, and only when it's sure. anything in between stays yours.

6. check in the morning. a morning review shows you what it did while you slept.

7. messages wait for you. max drafts openers in your voice, and nothing is sent without you.

it earns trust, and can lose it

autonomy is a ladder, not a switch.

the agent starts with no permission at all. after enough ratings, a small model is fitted on your mac, and auto-like and auto-pass unlock only if it predicts choices it was never shown better than chance. it acts only at the confident ends, under a daily cap, and if the model stops holding up, autopilot goes back off and the app tells you.

public var unlocksAgent: Bool {
    validationCount >= 50 && auc > 0.6
}

the unlock rule, from the code: at least 50 checked guesses, and better than a coin flip

a model graded on profiles it trained on can pass by memorizing you, so it's graded only on ones it never saw. you can't switch trust on. it has to be earned.

WingedMaxCore/RatingWeightPolicy.swift
/// A correction is the user deliberately overruling the agent,
/// which is the most informative thing they ever do. And taste
/// moves, so a rating from months ago describes who they were,
/// not who they are.
public func weight(ageDays: Double, corrected: Bool) -> Double {
    let decayed = pow(0.5, age / halfLifeDays)1
    let recency = max(decayed, minimumWeight)2
    return recency * (corrected ? correctionMultiplier : 1)3
}

what i bring from it

i design every agentic product by these now.

show the agent's conclusions, not just its inputs. if people train something, they need to see what it learned and correct it. that surface is the loop.

let the agent earn its freedom. start with no permission, unlock more only when it proves itself, and take it back when it doesn't.

only ask for input you'll use. every question you ask spends the goodwill training runs on.

honest state over optimistic state. "connected" means connected, and a counter counts the thing it names.

price the work up front. training takes about an hour of careful rating before anything changes, so the first screen says so.

if the agent concludes something about a person, show it to them and let them argue with it.
